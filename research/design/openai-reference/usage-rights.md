# Usage rights and brand boundary

This is an engineering risk classification, not legal advice.

The official [openai/apps-sdk-ui](https://github.com/openai/apps-sdk-ui) repository is MIT licensed. Preserve its copyright notice and license with copies or substantial portions. The local subset includes the original [LICENSE](assets/official/apps-sdk-ui-0.2.2-0f00143/LICENSE). Prefer the dependency to avoid a stale fork. MIT copyright permission does not grant trademark rights or permission to imply affiliation.

The [OpenAI Design Guidelines](https://openai.com/brand/) say OpenAI name/logo, ChatGPT/GPT brands, and other marks belong to OpenAI. They prohibit alteration, incorporation into another brand, confusingly similar logos, and implied endorsement; broader questions go to partnercomms@openai.com.

- Generic MIT Apps SDK UI tokens/components/icons: **safe-to-reuse under MIT**, with attribution and independent branding.
- OpenAI wordmark, Blossom, and ChatGPT/Codex/GPT/Sora/DALL·E marks: **do-not-use as Commons branding**.
- Logo/partnership downloads: **requires-permission/reference-only**.
- Marketing screenshots/video/animation: **reference-only by URL** absent a separate license.
- OpenAI Sans: **requires-permission**; no public redistribution license verified.

## Product-name risk

Codex Commons uses OpenAI's Codex mark in a product/repository name. The public guide gives no specific safe harbor. Combined with a Codex-like UI, it may look official. Before public launch, obtain trademark review or permission. A lower-confusion presentation is an independent Commons mark and factual “Commons for Codex” wording with a non-affiliation statement, subject to counsel.

The full brand portal requires legitimate human access. Review its font license, kit terms, and current naming rules; do not bypass access controls.
